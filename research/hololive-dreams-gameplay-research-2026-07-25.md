# hololive Dreams Gameplay Research

Checked: 2026-07-25

## Page decision

- Primary query: `hololive Dreams gameplay`
- Supporting intent: `how to play hololive Dreams`, `hololive Dreams difficulty`, and `hololive Dreams game modes`
- Route: `/hololive-dreams-gameplay`
- Navigation label: `Gameplay`
- Replaced route: `/hololive-dreams-steam-guide`

The page answers a broader player question than a platform setup page: what the
player actually does, how the main systems connect, which rhythm difficulties are
currently present, and what each mode contributes to progression.

## Confirmed gameplay model

The Steam product description and the current English game page support this loop:

1. Play Rhythm Lives and minigames.
2. Complete quests, including Holomem Quests.
3. Unlock playable talents and develop Dream Park facilities.
4. Train and organize the party.
5. Return to Rhythm Lives with stronger score potential.

The launch announcement also confirms that a new player can select one favorite
talent at the highest rarity during the opening selection.

## Rhythm difficulties

Current AppMedia song records display four difficulty columns:

- EASY
- NORMAL
- HARD
- EXPERT

The page therefore presents exactly four difficulties. A fifth `MASTER` tier was
excluded because it was not corroborated by the current song records or the
release-facing product material reviewed for this page.

The descriptions of when to use each difficulty are player recommendations, not
claims about hidden unlock rules, fixed note density, judgment windows, or rewards.

## Modes and features

Supported by the current Steam product description:

- Rhythm Live as the central play mode.
- Team training and party organization as part of score growth.
- Auto-play for players who do not want manual rhythm play.
- Create Chart for making and sharing original charts.
- Dream Park development and Holomem Quests.
- Solo minigames and supported multiplayer activities.
- Online PvP and online co-op feature support.
- Minigame rewards can accelerate talent growth and park development.

## Named minigames

- `Hoppin' Rope`: the current English game page describes this as a group jump-rope
  challenge with simple controls.
- `Colliect Cooking`: the spelling is retained from the English promotional
  presentation. Project-recorded stage footage shows ingredient-set matching and
  requested dishes under time pressure.
- `MEGA CIRCUIT`: project-recorded gameplay footage presents a competitive movement
  and obstacle course.
- `POKAJAN`: project research identifies it as a card-hand activity. The page avoids
  inventing detailed rules and tells players to read the current in-game rule screen.

## Editorial boundaries

- No external links are rendered on the gameplay page.
- No exact judgment windows, note counts, score formulas, unlock thresholds, or
  player-count limits are stated without current corroboration.
- Auto-play is presented as an available feature, not as proof that it completes
  every objective or earns identical rewards.
- Difficulty advice is framed as a practical learning route rather than a universal
  ranking of player skill.
- Multiplayer wording uses `supported` because availability depends on the selected
  minigame.
